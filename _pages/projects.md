Our research uses molecular modelling to uncover how chemical reactions proceed and why particular products are formed. We combine electronic-structure calculations, reaction-pathway exploration and chemical kinetics to address two connected questions: how molecular complexity emerges in space, and how the structure of a reacting system determines its reactivity and selectivity.

## 1. Astrochemistry

### Understanding how complex organic molecules form in space

How can complex organic molecules arise under the extreme conditions of the interstellar medium? We investigate the reaction pathways that connect simple interstellar species to increasingly complex organic structures, with particular interest in aromatic molecules and compounds relevant to prebiotic chemistry.

By combining automated reaction discovery with quantum-chemical calculations and kinetic analysis, we seek to identify viable formation mechanisms, assess competing pathways and establish which processes can operate under interstellar conditions. Complementary studies of molecular fragmentation help us understand how organic molecules break down, connecting their formation with questions of stability and survival. Our aim is to build a mechanistic understanding of chemical complexity in space and provide predictions that can guide laboratory studies and astronomical observations.

## 2. Mechanisms of organic and organometallic transformations

### Explaining reactivity and the origins of enantioselectivity

We use molecular modelling to understand how organic and organometallic reactions take place, from the first interaction between reactants to the formation of products. By characterising intermediates and transition states and comparing competing mechanisms, we investigate how catalysts, ligands and substrates shape the reaction pathway.

We are particularly interested in **enantioselective processes**: why does a reaction favour one enantiomer over the other, and which molecular interactions determine that preference? Our work examines the energetic and structural factors that govern reactivity, regioselectivity and stereoselectivity. In collaboration with experimental researchers, we aim to turn mechanistic insight into explanations of observed behaviour and principles that can inform the development of more selective transformations.
